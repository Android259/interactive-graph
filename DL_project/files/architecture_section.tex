\section{Architecture}

The model has two encoders, one per partner, a block in which the two read each other, a
pooling step that reduces each partner to a single vector, and a classifier over the
pair. Nothing inside it is pretrained. The two pretrained models of the previous section
enter as fixed tables of numbers, and every weight described below is trained from
scratch on the rows a split leaves in training.

The code carries a large number of alternatives at each of those stages. This section
describes the path the results below were obtained on, fixed by
\texttt{scripts/arg\_files/bbp\_dcs\_rnd\_smd\_fa\_nps\_dpt01\_gm\_plm8\_hid8\_wd001\_ep120.md},
and names an alternative where it is the natural point of comparison.

Three settings size everything below: the hidden width $d$ (\texttt{hiddim}), the number
of attention heads $h$ (\texttt{HEADS}), and the feed-forward multiplier $m$, which makes
every feed-forward $d \to m d \to d$. Both encoders, the block between them and the
classifier all run at $d$; the only widths that are not a multiple of it are the two frozen
embeddings on the way in. The reported runs set $d = 8$, $h = 8$ and $m = 4$, at which the
network holds 27286 parameters (Table~\ref{tab:params}), two thirds of them in the two
linear maps that compress the frozen embeddings down to $d$. The training splits the
network is fitted on hold between 921 and 1731 rows.

The two head counts are not the same kind of number. The three multi-head attention sites
split $d$ across $h$ heads, so at $d = h$ a head carries one dimension; the graph layer's
$h$ heads each emit $d$ and are concatenated, so it produces $h d$ and needs a projection
back.

\subsection{Lipid track}

The lipid enters as the frozen MolFormer token embeddings of the data section. A linear
map takes each token to $d$, and the tokens of one molecule are then mixed by a single
self-attention block -- normalise, attend, add the residual, normalise, feed-forward with
a LeakyReLU, add, normalise. The encoder normalises its output once more on the way out.
A second feed-forward between those two norms is available and is switched off in the
reported runs.

This track has no graph in it. The tokens of a molecule are complete to each other under
the attention, and the only thing the attention mask does is keep the tokens of one
sample away from the tokens of another in the same batch; the batch vector that PyTorch
Geometric carries alongside them is bookkeeping for that mask and for the pooling, not an
adjacency. Nothing here reads a bond, and nothing here carries stereochemistry: the
chemistry that reaches the classifier from this side is whatever MolFormer wrote into the
tokens of a canonicalised string, reweighted by attention.

\subsection{Protein track}

The graph the encoder reads is the residue-level Voronoi contact graph of the data
section; what this section adds to it is a direction and a width. Contacts are stored
once per pair and enter message passing in the direction they are stored in;
\texttt{bidirectional\_edges} appends the reverse of each and is off in the reported
runs. The 1536 ESM3 dimensions are compressed by a single linear layer before being
concatenated to the structural attributes and burial. Its output width is a setting of its
own (\texttt{plm\_compression\_dim}), which the reported runs set to $d$; that one layer
is 45\% of the parameter budget. A staged compression through three intermediate widths is
available and was not used here. Residue type reaches that layer as one ordinal number
from 0 to 19, alphabetical by one-letter code -- not a one-hot and not an embedding -- so
the amino-acid identity the network works with is the one ESM3 supplies.

One GATv2 layer \cite{gatv2} with the edge attributes produces the $h d$-wide
representation described above, which an MLP takes back to $d$. A second graph layer,
summed residually with the first, is available and off by default.
What follows is the same self-attention block as on the lipid side, with one addition. When
one residue decides how much attention to give another, a single trained number is added
to that score whenever the residue being read belongs to the cavity. It is the same
number for every such pair and it starts at one, so the encoder begins by reading the
binding site more closely than the rest of the chain, and training can raise that
preference, lower it, or cancel it. Nothing is removed: a residue outside the cavity is
read less, not never.

Two alternatives to the graph layer were built and run: GINE convolutions \cite{gine}
and a transformer convolution. A third, a geometric transformer operating on residue
frames rather than on scalar edge attributes, is implemented and has not been run.

\subsection{Cross-attention}

The two tracks meet in one block that runs both directions at once. Lipid nodes are
queries against protein nodes as keys and values, protein nodes are queries against lipid
nodes, and the two directions use the same pair of tensors with the roles swapped. Each
direction adds its result residually to its own stream, normalises, applies a
feed-forward, adds and normalises again \cite{vaswani}. The masks
allow attention only between nodes of the same pair, and the pocket bias of the previous
subsection is applied again in the direction where the lipid reads the protein, so what
the lipid takes from it is biased towards the cavity. The design follows the multimodal
antibody--antigen model of Cohen and Schneidman-Duhovny \cite{abagcross}, which combines
cross- and self-attention over two structures to build a representation of their complex.

The block leaves both streams at node level, and the reduction to one vector per partner
happens after it. The reported runs pool by generalised mean \cite{gem} with a learnable
exponent initialised at one, so pooling starts as an average and can move towards a
maximum during training.

The classifier is then an MLP over the concatenation of the two pooled vectors, $2d$
numbers, down through $m d$ and $d$ to two logits; the prediction is the larger of the two,
which is the 0.5 threshold on the positive class. At $d = 8$ that concatenation is sixteen
numbers, a narrow channel by construction: the concatenation lets the MLP reach a
decision from one partner alone, which on this dataset is the shortcut the data section's
reference predictors measure. Bilinear fusion replaces the concatenation with
$\mathrm{lip}^{\top} W \mathrm{prot}$, a product that no single-partner representation
survives, and is an ablation rather than the default.

\paragraph{Variants of the block.} A second round -- cross-attention, both encoders
again, cross-attention again -- is available under \texttt{double\_attention}. Three
gradient-reversal mechanisms \cite{ganin} hang off the same place: a per-partner adversary
that reads each pooled partner \emph{before} the cross-attention and is trained to predict
the label from it alone, so that the encoder is pushed to make one partner uninformative;
a family adversary that reads the fused vector \emph{after} it and is trained to name the
protein family, class-conditionally, so that what is aligned across families is the
representation given the label rather than the representation itself; and a chemistry
adversary against a frozen chemical prior. All three are off in the configuration reported
here, as is the frozen prior itself, which when enabled is fitted on training labels alone
before the network exists and added to the positive logit.

\paragraph{Attention layout.} A batch of graphs laid out as one long sequence with an
$N \times N$ mask computes far more than it keeps: measured on a real batch of sixteen,
the three attention sites together build 18.84M logits of which 2.09M are inside a graph.
\texttt{fast\_attention} computes the same quantity on a padded per-graph layout where the
blocked pairs do not exist, reusing the same projection weights. The reordering is not
bit-exact -- the two agree to about $6 \times 10^{-8}$ -- which is why it is a flag and not
a replacement. It is on in the reported runs.

\subsection{Training details}

\paragraph{Loss.} The prediction is binary and the loss is cross-entropy over the two
logits, weighted by class. The weights are the inverse class frequencies of the training
split, so at two sampled negatives per positive they are 0.75 and 1.5 -- the sampling
already carries most of the balancing, and the weights carry the remainder. Three
alternatives are implemented and reported as ablations: focal loss \cite{focal}, which
down-weights easy rows; the non-negative positive-unlabelled risk estimator \cite{nnpu},
which treats the zeros as unlabelled rather than negative and so matches how the labels
were actually produced; and a RankNet-style pairwise loss \cite{ranknet}, which asks
whether a positive row outranks a negative one instead of which side of a threshold each
row falls on, optionally restricted to pairs within one protein.

\paragraph{Optimisation.} Adam \cite{adam} at learning rate $10^{-4}$ with weight decay
0.01, 120 epochs, no learning-rate schedule; warm-up with cosine decay and stochastic
weight averaging are available and unused. Dropout is 0.1 in every feed-forward and in the
classifier. Batches hold sixteen rows and are drawn by a class-balanced sampler that
covers every training row once per epoch and takes its batch count from the larger class:
5.3 positive and 10.6 unlabelled rows per batch, and 58 to 109 batches per epoch depending
on the size of the block held out, so a run is between 6960 and 13080 optimiser steps.

\paragraph{What is reported.} Validation runs after every epoch, and the weights kept are
those of the epoch that maximises a five-epoch rolling mean of validation balanced
accuracy rather than the single best epoch, which on splits this small is frequently a
spike. Early stopping is disabled by default, so all 120 epochs run. The test split is
read once, with those weights, and never during training. Every figure is averaged over
five seeds, which change the drawn negatives and the halving of the block and nothing
else.

\paragraph{Cost.} Runs were made on a single Tesla V100-SXM2-32GB. At 27286 parameters an
epoch takes a median of 6.0 seconds and a full 120-epoch run about twelve minutes, so one
configuration over seven held-out families and five seeds is roughly seven GPU-hours. The
protein graphs and lipid encodings are built once per run and shared with the loader
workers copy-on-write; on the CRAL-TRIO block that cache is 35 protein graphs and 854
lipid encodings, 123\,MiB.
