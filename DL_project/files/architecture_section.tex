\section{Architecture}

The model has two encoders, one per partner, a block in which the two read each other, a
pooling step that reduces each partner to a single vector, and a classifier over the
pair. Nothing inside it is pretrained. The two pretrained models of the previous section
enter as fixed tables of numbers, and every weight described below is trained from
scratch on the rows a split leaves in training.

Three settings size the whole network: the hidden width $d$ (\texttt{hiddim}), the number
of attention heads $h$ (\texttt{HEADS}), and the feed-forward multiplier $m$, which makes
every feed-forward $d \to m d \to d$. Both encoders, the block between them and the
classifier all run at $d$; the only widths that are not a multiple of it are the two frozen
embeddings on the way in and their compression layers.

The two head counts are not the same kind of number. The three multi-head attention sites
split $d$ across $h$ heads, so each head carries $d / h$ dimensions; the graph layer's $h$
heads each emit $d$ and are concatenated, so it produces $hd$ and needs a projection back
to $d$.

\subsection{Lipid track}

The lipid enters as the frozen MolFormer token embeddings of the data section. A linear
map takes each token to $d$, and the tokens of one molecule are then mixed by a single
self-attention block -- normalise, attend, add the residual, normalise, feed-forward with
a LeakyReLU, add, normalise. The encoder normalises its output once more on the way out.

This track has no graph in it. The tokens of a molecule are complete to each other under
the attention, and the only thing the attention mask does is keep the tokens of one
sample away from the tokens of another in the same batch; the batch vector that PyTorch
Geometric carries alongside them is bookkeeping for that mask and for the pooling, not an
adjacency. Nothing here reads a bond, and nothing here carries stereochemistry: the
chemistry that reaches the classifier from this side is whatever MolFormer wrote into the
tokens of a canonicalised string, reweighted by attention.

When a row lists more than one candidate structure, drawing one of them
(\texttt{lipid\_random\_choice}) is marked on the training split alone: validation and
test always take the first-listed candidate and keep it fixed across epochs, so the
metric that selects the checkpoint and stops training early moves for the model rather
than for which molecule a row happened to show it. The draw itself is not cached --
caching it would freeze a row at whichever candidate a worker first drew, since
DataLoader workers persist across epochs -- so what is cached is the row's list of
canonical candidate keys, and a live draw costs one dictionary lookup rather than a
re-canonicalisation.

\subsection{Protein track}

The graph the encoder reads is the residue-level Voronoi contact graph of the data
section; what this section adds to it is a direction and a width. Contacts are stored
once per pair and enter message passing in the direction they are stored in. The 1536
ESM3 dimensions are compressed by a linear layer before being concatenated to the
structural attributes and burial; its output width is a free parameter of its own,
independent of $d$ on the way in. Residue type reaches that layer as one ordinal number
from 0 to 19, alphabetical by one-letter code -- not a one-hot and not an embedding -- so
the amino-acid identity the network works with is the one ESM3 supplies.

A GATv2 layer \cite{gatv2} with the edge attributes produces the $hd$-wide
representation described above, which an MLP takes back to $d$. What follows is the same
self-attention block as on the lipid side, with one addition. When one residue decides
how much attention to give another, a single trained number is added to that score
whenever the residue being read belongs to the cavity. It is the same number for every
such pair and it starts at one, so the encoder begins by reading the binding site more
closely than the rest of the chain, and training can raise that preference, lower it, or
cancel it. Nothing is removed: a residue outside the cavity is read less, not never.

\subsection{Cross-attention, pooling and classification}

The two tracks meet in one block that runs both directions at once. Lipid nodes are
queries against protein nodes as keys and values, protein nodes are queries against lipid
nodes, and the two directions use the same pair of tensors with the roles swapped. Each
direction adds its result residually to its own stream, normalises, applies a
feed-forward, adds and normalises again \cite{vaswani}. The masks
allow attention only between nodes of the same pair, and the pocket bias of the previous
subsection is applied again in the direction where the lipid reads the protein, so what
the lipid takes from it is biased towards the cavity. The design follows the multimodal
antibody--antigen model of Cohen and Schneidman-Duhovny \cite{abagcross}, which combines
cross- and self-attention over two structures to build a representation of their complex.
This block and each encoder's
self-attention -- three sites in all -- are each a
\texttt{torch.nn.MultiheadAttention} call over every node of every graph in the batch,
both partners concatenated into one flat sequence of length $N$ and masked $N \times N$
to block scores between nodes of different graphs. That costs two things. The full $N
\times N$ matrix is built per head regardless of the mask: on a real batch of sixteen the
three sites build 18.84M scores this way, of which 2.09M, the block-diagonal, survive,
and that gap grows quadratically in $N$ as more graphs join the batch while the
block-diagonal grows only linearly. And the mask itself, shaped $(N \cdot h, L, S)$ as
\texttt{nn.MultiheadAttention} requires it, is materialised once per head although it
does not depend on the head.

The block leaves both streams at node level, and a pooling step reduces each to a single
$d$-wide vector per partner. The classifier is then an MLP over the concatenation of the
two pooled vectors, $2d$ numbers, down through $md$ and $d$ to two logits; the prediction
is the larger of the two, which is the 0.5 threshold on the positive class. That
concatenation is a narrow channel by construction: it lets the MLP reach a decision from
one partner alone, which on this dataset is the shortcut the data section's reference
predictors measure.

\paragraph{Attention over a batch of graphs.} The same three sites are computed instead through
\texttt{torch.nn.functional.scaled\_dot\_product\_attention}, on a dense
$(\text{graphs}, \text{max nodes}, d)$ layout where a pair of nodes from different
graphs is absent and not zeroed, and the mask broadcasts over heads rather than
being copied for each. The pocket bias saves the same way -- it depends on the key
alone, not on the query reading it, so it is built once as $(\text{graphs}, h, 1,
\text{max keys})$ and broadcast over queries, 2.3K entries here against 2.65M for a bias
sized to every query. The projection weights are identical; only the
arithmetic's order changes, which shifts floating-point rounding enough that the two
paths agree to about $6 \times 10^{-8}$ absolute, not bitwise.

\subsection{Alternatives measured against this architecture}

The code carries a second graph layer summed residually with the first, a second
feed-forward after each self-attention block, a staged compression of the ESM3 embedding
through intermediate widths instead of one linear layer, and a reversed copy of every
protein contact edge -- each an alternative sizing or wiring of a module described above,
switchable independently of the others. Three different graph convolutions can stand in
for the GATv2 layer: GINE convolutions \cite{gine}, a transformer convolution, and a
geometric transformer operating on residue frames rather than on scalar edge attributes.
Pooling can be a learnable generalised mean \cite{gem}, a maximum, an attention-weighted
sum, or a sliced-Wasserstein embedding; the classifier's concatenation of the two pooled
vectors can be replaced by a bilinear product $\mathrm{lip}^{\top} W \mathrm{prot}$, which
no single-partner representation survives, unlike the concatenation.

A second round -- cross-attention, both encoders again, cross-attention again -- is
available as \texttt{double\_attention}. Three gradient-reversal mechanisms
\cite{ganin} hang off the cross-attention block: a per-partner adversary that reads each
pooled partner \emph{before} the cross-attention and is trained to predict the label from
it alone, so that the encoder is pushed to make one partner uninformative; a family
adversary that reads the fused vector \emph{after} it and is trained to name the protein
family, class-conditionally, so that what is aligned across families is the
representation given the label rather than the representation itself; and a chemistry
adversary against a frozen chemical prior, which when enabled is fitted on training
labels alone before the network exists and added to the positive logit.

A row with more than one candidate structure can also be read whole instead of by the
one-candidate draw: \texttt{lipid\_concat} lays every candidate's tokens end to end into
one sequence the self-attention mixes freely, and \texttt{fragments\_mask} does the same
but keeps a per-candidate id so the self-attention can be restricted to each candidate's
own tokens, encoding every candidate of a row in one forward pass instead of one per
access.

Three loss functions are implemented besides the weighted cross-entropy the classifier's
logits feed by default: focal loss \cite{focal}, which down-weights easy rows; the
non-negative positive-unlabelled risk estimator \cite{nnpu}, which treats the zeros as
unlabelled rather than negative and so matches how the labels were actually produced; and
a RankNet-style pairwise loss \cite{ranknet}, which asks whether a positive row outranks a
negative one instead of which side of a threshold each row falls on, optionally
restricted to pairs within one protein.

\section{Experimental configurations}

This section describes the path the results below were obtained on, fixed by
\texttt{scripts/arg\_files/bbp\_dcs\_rnd\_smd\_fa\_nps\_dpt01\_gm\_plm8\_hid8\_wd001\_ep120.md},
and where each alternative of the previous section stands: on, off, or not run.

The reported runs set $d = 8$, $h = 8$ and $m = 4$, at which the network holds 27286
parameters (Table~\ref{tab:params}), two thirds of them in the two linear maps that
compress the frozen embeddings down to $d$. At $d = h = 8$ each attention head carries
exactly one dimension. The training splits the network is fitted on hold between 879 and
1713 rows.

\paragraph{Track and block alternatives.} Off in the reported runs: the second
feed-forward after each self-attention block, the second graph layer, the staged ESM3
compression (a single $1536 \to 8$ linear layer is used instead), \texttt{bidirectional\_edges},
\texttt{double\_attention}, all three gradient-reversal adversaries and the frozen
chemistry prior, and bilinear fusion of the pooled vectors. Of the three alternative
graph convolutions, GINE convolutions and the transformer convolution were both run as
ablations; the geometric transformer is implemented and has not been run. Pooling is a
generalised mean with a learnable exponent initialised at one, so pooling starts as an
average and can move towards a maximum during training; attention pooling and the
sliced-Wasserstein alternative were not used.

\paragraph{Attention layout.} The grouped, per-graph computation of the previous section
is gated behind \texttt{fast\_attention} rather than replacing the direct masked layout,
because the two are not bit-exact -- and it is on in the reported runs.

\paragraph{Loss.} The three alternative loss functions of the previous section are all
reported as ablations. The default they are measured against is cross-entropy over the
two logits, weighted by class: the weights are the inverse class frequencies of the
training split, so at two sampled negatives per positive they are 0.75 and 1.5 -- the
sampling already carries most of the balancing, and the weights carry the remainder.

\paragraph{Optimisation.} Adam \cite{adam} at learning rate $10^{-4}$ with weight decay
0.01, 120 epochs, no learning-rate schedule; warm-up with cosine decay and stochastic
weight averaging are available and unused. Dropout is 0.1 in every feed-forward and in the
classifier. Batches hold sixteen rows and are drawn by a class-balanced sampler that
covers every training row once per epoch and takes its batch count from the training
split's own size: 5.3 positive and 10.6 unlabelled rows per batch, and 55 to 108 batches
per epoch depending on the family held out, so a run is between 6600 and 12960 optimiser
steps.

\paragraph{What is reported.} Validation runs after every epoch, and the weights kept are
those of the epoch that maximises a five-epoch rolling mean of validation balanced
accuracy rather than the single best epoch, which on splits this small is frequently a
spike. Early stopping is disabled by default, so all 120 epochs run. The test split is
read once, with those weights, and never during training. Every figure is averaged over
five seeds, which change the drawn negatives and the halving of the block and nothing
else.

\paragraph{Cost.} Runs were made on a single Tesla V100-SXM2-32GB. At 27286 parameters an
epoch takes a median of 6.0 seconds and a full 120-epoch run about twelve minutes, so one
configuration over seven held-out families and five seeds is roughly seven GPU-hours. The
protein graphs and lipid encodings are built once per run and shared with the loader
workers copy-on-write; on the CRAL-TRIO block that cache is 35 protein graphs and 854
lipid encodings, 123\,MiB.
