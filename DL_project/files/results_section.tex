Balanced accuracy does not calibrate a threshold on this split: a chemistry-only
predictor that ignores the protein and scores a lipid by the training positive rate of

alone is credited for where its decision boundary sits, not for what it knows. Everything
past this point is therefore reported as AUC, against the same chemistry-only null model,
computed on the identical rows.

\subsection{Ranking against the chemistry null model}

Two questions are conflated by an AUC taken over the whole block: whether a lipid binds
anything at all, which needs no protein and is what the null model answers, and whether
it binds \emph{this} protein, which is the term the network exists to add. Pairs between
two different proteins outnumber pairs within one by an order of magnitude, so the
pooled AUC over a block answers mostly the first question. The same AUC computed inside
one protein and averaged over proteins removes it by construction: every row of one
protein shares the same partner, so only the pair is left to rank by. Both are reported,
$k = 15$ nearest neighbours for the null model.

The network exceeds the null model on exactly two of seven families in validation, GLTP
and scp2, and on exactly one in test, GLTP -- the same two columns, pooled and

are not the same. Every other family ranks below the chemistry-only model on both

IP\_trans by as much as 0.408 pooled in validation. In-protein, the null model itself
reaches 0.575--0.733 on the three families with the most chemically familiar blocks
(IP\_trans, LBP\_BPI\_CETP, scp2), which is the residual similarity-based leak
Section~\ref{sec:splits} measures rather than removes; a network below that line is
ranking the block worse than nearest-neighbour chemistry alone.

\subsection{Increment over chemistry}

An upper bound on what the network adds to the chemistry score is a logistic fit of the
label on the standardised chemistry score alone, then on chemistry plus the network's
score, both fit \emph{on the block itself}: if the network cannot beat chemistry even
with a coefficient chosen from the block's own labels, it has nothing left to add under
any calibration learned elsewhere.

\caption{In-sample increment of AUC-equivalent fit quality, network plus chemistry over

crediting the network for chemistry it already had access to, not for a pair term.}
\label{tab:randincrement}
\end{table}

Four families hold a positive in-protein increment on both splits: CRAL-TRIO, GLTP,
lipocalin, START. On three of them -- CRAL-TRIO, lipocalin, START -- Tables
\ref{tab:randaucvalid}--\ref{tab:randauctest} already show the network's own in-protein
AUC ranking \emph{below} the chemistry null model on both splits. The increment is an
in-sample fit free to assign the network's score any coefficient, including a negative
one, so a positive value here does not corroborate the raw ranking; it is consistent with
the fit absorbing block-specific noise, which is exactly the caveat an in-sample upper
bound carries. GLTP is the one family where the two measurements agree with each other:
its in-protein AUC exceeds chemistry on both splits (Tables
\ref{tab:randaucvalid}--\ref{tab:randauctest}) and so does its increment -- but its block
trains on a single positive of any kind (Table~\ref{tab:doublesplit}) and 2 usable
proteins, so the agreement is read as a near-degenerate block, not as evidence of a pair
term. scp2, the only family a pooled-AUC comparison on an earlier, non-deduplicated
version of this table had reported as a reproducible positive case, holds a positive
in-protein increment on validation (+0.020, matching its raw AUC there) and a negative
one on test ($-$0.018, also matching). IP\_trans and LBP\_BPI\_CETP are the two families
where raw AUC is clearly below chemistry on both splits and the increment does not
contradict it: LBP\_BPI\_CETP's is negative on both ($-$0.012, $-$0.002), IP\_trans's is
within 0.013 of zero on both rather than positive.

\subsection{Conclusion}

At a fixed threshold the network exceeds the chemistry-blind reference by 0.045 balanced
accuracy on average and by no more than 0.101 on any family; ranked in-protein against a
chemistry-only null model that requires no protein, it exceeds that model, mean over two
seeds, on two of seven families in validation (GLTP, scp2) and one in test (GLTP), and no
family on both splits. scp2's own per-seed, per-epoch trajectory shows why: both seeds
learn something on validation, climbing from clearly negative to a stable positive
plateau by epoch 49, but neither reproduces that trajectory on test, one decaying to
zero and the other staying negative throughout. GLTP's two usable proteins and single
training positive of any kind leave its margin unread as evidence of a pair term. On this panel,
at this split, no family yet shows a pairwise lipid--protein signal beyond what chemical
similarity to the training positives, or a near-degenerate block, already supplies.
