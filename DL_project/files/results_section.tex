A network compared by BA alone is credited for where its decision boundary sits, not for
what it knows. Everything past this point is therefore reported as AUC, against a
chemistry-only null model, computed on the identical rows.

\subsection{Ranking against the chemistry null model}

A chemistry-only predictor that ignores the protein and scores a lipid by the training
positive rate of its $k = 15$ nearest neighbours reaches BA 0.512 at a threshold fit on training
while ranking the same rows at AUC 0.565--0.589 (Section~\ref{sec:splits}). Two questions
are conflated by an AUC taken over the whole block: whether a lipid binds
anything at all, which needs no protein and is what the null model answers, and whether
it binds \emph{this} protein, which is the term the network exists to add. Pairs between
two different proteins outnumber pairs within one by an order of magnitude, so the
pooled AUC over a block answers mostly the first question. The same AUC computed inside
one protein and averaged over proteins removes it by construction: every row of one
protein shares the same partner, so only the pair is left to rank by.

GLTP is the family that comes closest to a consistent win: the network exceeds the null
model pooled in validation (by 0.001), pooled in test (by 0.060) and in-protein in test
(by 0.035), and is a near-tie in-protein in validation (0.500 to 0.501, 0.001 below).
scp2 exceeds the null model only in-protein in validation, by 0.072. No other
family-column-split combination favours the network: the closest miss is START pooled
in validation, 0.005 below, and the largest gap is IP\_trans pooled in validation, 0.297
below. In-protein, the null model itself reaches 0.519--0.714 on the three families with
the most chemically familiar blocks (IP\_trans, LBP\_BPI\_CETP, scp2), which is the
residual similarity-based leak Section~\ref{sec:splits} measures but does not remove; a
network below that line is ranking the block worse than nearest-neighbour chemistry
alone.

\subsection{Increment over chemistry}

An upper bound on what the network adds to the chemistry score is a logistic fit of the
label on the standardised chemistry score alone, then on chemistry plus the network's
score, both fit \emph{on the block itself}: if the network cannot beat chemistry even
with a coefficient chosen from the block's own labels, it has nothing left to add under
any calibration learned elsewhere.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering
\begin{tabular}{lrr|rr}
\hline
& \multicolumn{2}{c}{validation} & \multicolumn{2}{c}{test} \\
family & pooled & in-protein & pooled & in-protein \\
\hline
CRAL-TRIO & +0.104 & +0.047 & +0.134 & +0.080 \\
GLTP & +0.087 & +0.076 & +0.032 & +0.019 \\
IP\_trans & +0.051 & +0.025 & +0.014 & +0.013 \\
LBP\_BPI\_CETP & $-$0.001 & $-$0.007 & +0.004 & +0.003 \\
lipocalin & +0.128 & +0.074 & +0.153 & +0.111 \\
scp2 & +0.054 & +0.022 & +0.025 & +0.001 \\
START & +0.042 & +0.019 & +0.054 & +0.049 \\
\hline
\end{tabular}
\caption{In-sample increment of AUC-equivalent fit quality, network plus chemistry over
chemistry alone, epoch 120, mean over five seeds. A positive pooled entry can still be
the marginal: lipocalin and CRAL-TRIO show large pooled increments while their
in-protein networks rank below chemistry (Tables~\ref{tab:randaucvalid}--\ref{tab:randauctest}),
so the pooled fit is crediting the network for chemistry it already had access to, not
for a pair term.}
\label{tab:randincrement}
\end{table}

Six of the seven families hold a positive in-protein increment on both splits; only
LBP\_BPI\_CETP does not, at $-$0.007 valid and +0.003 test, both within noise of zero.
For four of those six -- CRAL-TRIO, IP\_trans, lipocalin, START -- Tables
\ref{tab:randaucvalid}--\ref{tab:randauctest} already show the network's own in-protein
AUC ranking \emph{below} the chemistry null model on both splits. The increment is an
in-sample fit free to assign the network's score any coefficient, including a negative
one, so a positive value here does not corroborate the raw ranking; it is consistent with
the fit absorbing block-specific noise, which is exactly the caveat an in-sample upper
bound carries; with five seeds this is now the modal pattern, not an exception limited to
three families. GLTP and scp2 are the two families whose increment sign matches their
raw ranking on at least one split. GLTP's in-protein AUC exceeds chemistry on test
(Table~\ref{tab:randauctest}) with a matching positive increment there (+0.019); on
validation its AUC is a near-tie against chemistry ($-$0.001) while its increment is
+0.076, so the two do not agree in validation the way they do in test. Its block also
trains on a single positive of any kind (Table~\ref{tab:doublesplit}) and 2 usable
proteins, so even the test agreement is read as a near-degenerate block, not as evidence
of a pair term. scp2's in-protein AUC exceeds chemistry on validation (+0.072) with a
matching increment (+0.022); on test both collapse toward zero -- AUC 0.152 below
chemistry, increment +0.001 -- neither corroborating nor contradicting each other.
LBP\_BPI\_CETP remains the one family whose raw AUC stays clearly below chemistry on
both splits without a compensating positive increment.

\subsection{Conclusion}

On this panel, at this split, no family yet shows a pairwise lipid--protein signal
beyond what chemical similarity to the training positives, or a near-degenerate block,
already supplies.
